# 4D design contract

## World
- World space is R^4 with coordinates (x, y, z, w); y is up and gravity is -y. Physics (`physics_solver.hpp`) already runs on `glm::vec4` and stays in world space: rotating the view never changes physics.
- Every visible thing is a 4D shape with a signed distance function (SDF) in R^4: fruit collide as hyperspheres (glomes) and look like their fruit (4D bodies, stems, leaves and crowns within 1.6 radii, with irregular silhouettes, raised rind detail, and a grape that is a packed 4D cluster of berries; see `fruit4d.glsl`), the bowl is a hand-thrown 4D ceramic bowl (wobbling lip, uneven wall, throwing ridges, foot ring, crackled glaze with drips, all varying over the 2-sphere of directions around its axis) whose inner surface is the physics hemisphere to within 0.004 units (0.14% of its radius), the island is a 4D terrain plus 4D rocks, trees (spherinder trunks, glome canopies), fences, pond, and clouds. The sky is a function of 4D direction.

## Camera and rotation
- The camera has a position p in R^4 and an orientation R in SO(4) (an orthonormal 4x4 matrix, re-orthonormalized after updates). Its first three columns span the 3D slice the player sees; the fourth is the slice normal.
- What the player sees is the 3D cross-section of the world by the hyperplane through p spanned by R's first three columns. Rotating in the xw, yw and zw planes turns that hyperplane, so the whole scene changes shape; rotating in xy, xz and yz is ordinary 3D turning.
- Controls must make 4D rotation reachable and reversible: a clear "back to the default slice" action, and a small on-screen indicator of the slice's orientation and offset along its normal.
- Implementation (`src/4d_game/camera4d.hpp`): R = slice * orbit. `orbit` is ordinary yaw and pitch inside the slice (right-drag or arrow keys; the mouse turns into 4D with middle-drag or Shift+right-drag, and the wheel turns the slice or sets the drop's w, see `docs/controls.md`); `slice` holds every 4D turn and the roll, stored as a pair of unit quaternions so composition, re-orthonormalization and slerp are exact. The camera orbits the bowl: p = target + offset * R[3] + distance * R[2].
- Keys turn in the camera's own planes, so they act on what the player sees: A/D xw, R/F yw, Q/E zw, Z/C roll (xy), W/S move the slice along its normal, H or Space eases back to the default slice. Turns ease in and out, and opposite keys undo each other exactly.
- The indicator (bottom-left) draws each world axis as it appears in the slice: x red, y green, z blue, w gold. An axis tilted out of the slice shrinks and its tip grows a ring, so in the default slice w is a ring at the centre. A gold marker on the bar beside it shows the offset; the text gives the tilt from the default slice in degrees.
- The bottom w strip (V, or Settings) shows every fruit along the current slice normal, which is world w in the default orientation. Fruit markers show their radius along that axis and are arranged vertically by world height. The bright vertical line marks the current slice, and a triangle marks the held fruit. Gameplay opens with a closer, lower orbit and score and next-drop panels across the top.

## Rendering
- One fullscreen fragment shader raymarches the 4D SDF scene. Each pixel's ray starts at p and runs along R * (ray direction in slice coordinates, 0), so rays stay inside the slice.
- Shading uses a 4D sun direction, soft shadows and ambient occlusion, and fog. Faceted low-poly shading (quantized normals) gives the cover art's look.
- Lighting is slice-relative for the world: its normal is the 4D gradient projected into the slice (the normal of the cross-section the player sees), and shadow and AO rays run inside the slice (the sun projected into it). So what casts a shadow is what the player can see, and shading reads as plain 3D shading of the visible cross-section. Fruit shading keeps the full 4D normal. Leaves pass some sun through (wrap lighting) so canopies seen from below stay green, as on the cover.
- Every fruit the slice cuts wears a depth ring: a band about 6 px wide round its cross-section (constant on screen, whatever the fruit's size or distance) with a dark inner edge so it reads on any fruit colour. Ring colour follows the signed distance of the centre from the slice, going from gold on the slice to warm coral on the negative side or blue on the positive side, fully shifted at 0.7 units. The w strip uses the same colours.
- The ceramic bowl is opaque. Its lit rim is a crisp cream line where the rounded lip catches the sun and sky, and the only glow on the bowl is a faint warm band at the lip (a third of the old body glow; it turns red when the bowl is full). In slice views only pixels where a fruit is behind the near shell and within the bowl cavity reveal that fruit, shaded at its own depth, and the wall's cut face around that opening is a thin lit line (about 2 px at 1080p, compose pass only). Fog begins 20 units behind the camera target and thickens slowly, so the hill round the bowl stays clear.
- Cutaway: world surfaces within `clamp(distance - 7, 0, 5)` of the camera are not drawn (a ray starting inside a solid there walks out first), so a hill that lives at some w cannot swallow the camera when the slice turns.
- Merge glow (Settings > Merge glow, `--no-glow`; on by default): a fruit whose nearest same-kind fruit is within 1.0 units in 4D (gap between the glomes, whatever the slice shows) gets a pulsing light rim on its own surface, stronger and faster the closer the partner. A partner off the slice shows its hint ring at full strength, so the player sees where to turn to bring the pair together.
- Depth hints for things just off the slice (a faint ghost of nearby fruit, tinted by their offset along the slice normal) are allowed; they must read as hints, not solid objects.
- Implemented as a dashed depth ring with a faint fill where the fruit's centre projects into the slice, using the same signed colour as solid rings. Strength fades to zero 1.5 units beyond the slice (16 nearest), and hints behind scene geometry are dimmer.
- Measured on the development Mac at 1080p High (wall time per frame with `glFinish`, medians): 12.6 ms in play (6 or 20 fruit), 15.3 ms while the slice turns every frame, 15.0 ms on the start menu. Before the slice cache it was 34–48 ms.
- The same GLSL serves the desktop build and the web build (WebGL2 through emscripten). Keep shaders within GLSL ES 3.00 features.
- Shader files carry no `#version`; the host prepends it (330 core or 300 es), defines `MAX_FRUIT`, and pastes `#include "world4d.glsl"`.
- `resources/shaders/world4d.glsl` (world) provides `vec2 worldSDF(vec4 p)` (distance, material id), `vec3 skyColor(vec4 dir)` and `vec3 materialColor(float id, vec4 p, vec4 n)`; it may read `uTime` and `uSunDir`. Ids: 1 ground, 2 bowl, 10+ world props; fruit are drawn by the renderer.
- For the slice cache the world is split: `vec2 worldStaticSDF(vec4 p)` is everything that never moves and must not read `uTime`, all of it inside the glome `WORLD_STATIC_CENTER` / `WORLD_STATIC_RADIUS` (`#define`s the host also reads); `vec2 worldDynamicSDF(vec4 p, float best)` is what moves (clouds), returning `best` when nothing is nearer; `worldSDF` is their union. `vec2 worldStaticSDFFor(vec4 p, float mat)` evaluates only the parts that can carry material `mat` (for normals at a hit), and `float bowlSDF(vec4 p)` defines the bowl surface. All SDFs must be 1-Lipschitz (true distances or underestimates), which the cache's step bound relies on.
- `resources/shaders/fruit4d.glsl` (world) provides the fruit look. `q` is the point minus the fruit centre rotated into that fruit's local SO(4) frame, `r` its current radius, `lod` 0..1 its on-screen detail: `float fruitSDF(vec4 q, float r, float type, float lod)`, `vec3 fruitAlbedo(vec4 q, vec4 n, float r, float type, float seed, float lod)` (linear; `n` is local), `vec3 fruitBaseColor(float type)` (one linear colour, for hints and the drop preview) and `float fruitGloss(float type)`. Everything lies within `FRUIT_BOUND` radii of the centre; the body stays close to the collision glome. Stems, leaves and crowns are 4D shapes (dropped below lod 0.35), thickened along the fruit's w so a turned slice still shows them; stems start inside the body and are thicker in w than leaves, so a leaf never floats free of its stem. The grape is a bunch of 30 berries on the D4 lattice (the densest 4D ball packing, each berry touching 24 others), tapering downward in four layers, so turning the slice reshuffles which berries show and how large; surface patterns are functions of the 4D local point, so they stay coherent under rotation and fade with lod. It is included after `world4d.glsl` and reuses its helpers.
- Fruit reach the shader as `uFruitCount`, `uFruitCenters[MAX_FRUIT]` (world vec4), `uFruitData[MAX_FRUIT]` (radius, type, flash, facet/variation seed = slot id + 1) and `uFruitWorldToLocal[MAX_FRUIT]` (SO(4) mat4 that rotates `p - centre` into the fruit frame). Game keeps the sphere colliders in world space, gives each spawned fruit a random SO(4) frame, and integrates a six-plane angular velocity bivector from rolling fruit/bowl contacts with damping. Game passes all fruit to `RaymarchRenderer::setFruit`; the renderer keeps those whose glome crosses the slice, nearest first, up to 64 on the lab WebGL2 target.
- Facets quantize the 4D normal to the D4 lattice (24-cell Voronoi cells), so they are fixed to world orientation, cost no extra SDF work, and change as the slice turns. `uFacet` blends smooth to faceted.
- Fruit: the ray enters each fruit's `FRUIT_BOUND` glome analytically, then sphere-traces `fruitSDF` inside it (at most 24 steps); normals come from its 4D gradient. `lod` is smoothstep(6, 24) of the fruit's screen radius in pixels; at lod 0 the fruit is its plain glome, hit analytically. Fruit shadows and AO stay analytic glomes. A soft fill from the viewer keeps back-lit fruit readable. Cost on top of the world: about 1.3 ms at 1080p High with 14 fruit.
- `4d_game --headless --mute --fruit-sheet out.png` renders all 11 fruit, labelled, at tilt 0 and after a camera-local turn (`--sheet-turn`, default yw:35); every fruit PR shows it.
- The review turntable (`--turntable dir`, `make turntable`; docs/recording.md) shows one fruit, or the bowl, alone and centred while the slice turns 0 to 180° through xw, then yw, then zw, pivoting on the subject's 4D centre. The host compiles the shaders with `#define TURNTABLE 1` (no world) or `2` (the bowl only); `world4d.glsl` honours it in `worldStaticSDF`, `worldDynamicSDF` and `worldShadowSDF`, and the renderer draws a fixed backdrop instead of the sky. Fruit and bowl PRs show its contact sheets.
- Slice cache: the static world is baked for the current slice into a 3D distance texture (R16F, a cube around the ball where the static bound glome meets the slice; 96, 128, 160 or 192 texels a side by quality preset). It is rebaked only when the slice turns or moves (one draw per layer, about 4 ms at 160³): at half resolution (an eighth of the work) while it keeps moving, then once at full resolution when it holds still. Orbiting and zooming never rebake, since they stay within the slice. The primary ray clips to the cube and steps by the cached distance minus 0.9 texel (trilinear filtering of a 1-Lipschitz field is within 0.87 texel of the truth); within a texel of a surface it switches to the analytic `worldStaticSDF`, so thin parts are never missed and hits are exact. Shadow and AO rays read only the cache. The moving world is marched live with capped steps. Normals take three forward differences of `worldStaticSDFFor`. Without float render targets (some WebGL2 devices) the renderer falls back to marching the analytic SDF, with `worldShadowSDF` (if the world `#define`s `WORLD_SHADOW_SDF`) for shadows and AO.
- The scene is marched into an offscreen target at a render scale, then a present pass upscales it and draws the indicator at full resolution.
- The scene runs as three passes from one shader (`PASS` 1–3): geometry traces fruit and world and writes the hit (distance, material or fruit, lod) and smooth normal to a float G-buffer; lighting computes shadow and AO at half resolution; compose shades every pixel, taking light from the four nearest half-resolution texels weighted by depth similarity, so it never bleeds across silhouettes. Each program is smaller than the single-pass one (`PASS` 0, the fallback without float targets and the `--cost-map` path), and the split alone made 1080p High about 20% faster.
- Performance: the scene passes are timed with GPU timer queries, and resolution adapts towards a 13 ms scene budget, between 35% and all of the quality preset's cap. Presets cap the marched pixel count (Low 960×540, Medium 1280×720, High 1920×1080, Ultra native) and set march, shadow and AO budgets; G cycles them, F3 shows the timing. The web build has no timer queries and adapts from frame time.
- `4d_game --screenshot out.png` and `--bench N` run headless (hidden window, no focus, no Dock icon) for PR screenshots and timing; see `main.cpp` for camera and demo flags. `--bench` reports per-frame wall time with `glFinish` (median and fastest quartile) besides the GPU timer, `--bench-turn deg` turns the slice every frame so the cache rebakes, and `--no-cache` compares with the analytic march, `--single-pass` with the one-pass scene. `--playtest-seconds s --playtest-render dir` renders the scripted playtest (every 10th frame, timed, a frame saved every 30 s, per-frame times in `times.csv`), and `--pose-at t` sets the playtest's camera pose at time t for a screenshot or bench. `--cost-map out.png` counts SDF evaluations per pixel (static world, moving world, fruit) instead of drawing colour: a deterministic profile when other work shares the GPU. Wrap GPU benchmarks in `/tmp/4dsuika-gpulock` so seats do not measure over each other.

## View modes
The slice view is the game.
One other view exists so the player can compare; Slice is the default (the PI's choice) and 4D eye leaves the slice view unchanged. A thick-slice (x-ray ghost) view was tried and removed at the PI's word.
**M** switches between Slice and 4D eye; Settings has **View** (the same). A small label bottom-left always names the view and the key (`view: Slice   (M changes)`). The choice is saved in `~/.4dsuika-view` (native builds) and restored on the next run; with no file the game starts in Slice. Headless runs take `--view slice|eye`.
- 4D eye: a 4D camera sees the scene on a 3D retina, as a 3D eye sees it on a 2D one.
  Retina coordinates (u, v, s) look along the camera's forward axis tilted by u right, v up and s along the slice normal, so the s = 0 plane is exactly the slice view's image (through a lens 0.55 times as wide, so the bowl fills the retina).
  Each retina texel is one 4D ray: fruit show whole, at every s where a ray hits them, instead of as cross-sections.
  Rays trace the fruit and the world's coarse occluder surface (`worldShadowSDF`, with materials from `worldSDF` at the hit), shaded without shadows. The bowl is translucent in the experimental retina display.
  The retina (High: 108 rows, 64 layers in s, RGBA8, one draw per four layers) is shown as a semi-transparent volume: a box with u and v as on screen and s in depth, seen from a little right of and above its s axis, marched front to back with density 1 for fruit and 0.008 for the world (0.02 the bowl).
  On the development Mac it costs less than the slice view.
  The cursor still aims through the slice, so playing in this view is for looking, not aiming.
- `4d_game --headless --mute --view-compare dir` (or `make view-compare`) renders the comparison.
  It covers every view and every combination: slice and 4D eye, each alone and with game's w-depth strip (`+inset`), four in all.
  One run of the scripted playtest drives all of them, so every view shows the same game.
  Each view gets a still, `view-<v>.png`, of three moments: 15 s, 90 s, and the next merge after that, with the slice moved through the new fruit.
  `compare.png` stacks all four.
  Each view also gets a 10 s clip of real play from 88 s, `view-<v>.mp4`, with the slice swinging through xw and yw on top of the scripted camera.

## Game
- Fruit drop on the bowl from a cursor in the 3D slice; the drop point is mapped to world space through R and p, so the player can aim anywhere in 4D by rotating.
- W-lane snap (L, or Settings, `--lane-snap`; off by default) moves the drop along world w to the nearest lane, a whole `kLaneSpacing` (1.0) from the bowl's centre, or to the next lane toward the centre if that one would fall outside the bowl. The aim is then a choice of lane: the w strip draws the lanes as they lie along the slice normal, the held-fruit triangle sits on the chosen lane, and a gold tag under the next-drop panel says the snap is on.
- A dashed guide projects the world-gravity drop path and marks predicted first contact with the bowl or the current fruit pile. The drop ghost and the landing ghost are the next fruit itself: its own SDF in the orientation it will drop with (chosen when it becomes the next fruit, and a falling fruit does not spin until it touches something), translucent, cut by the current slice, and seen through the bowl's near wall. So the outline is the real silhouette, and with the slice turned a fruit that starts off the slice shows only the part that reaches it. The landing ghost is a stationary-collider estimate, so falling and settling fruit can change the contact.
- Effects (Settings > Effects, `--no-fx`; on by default) are cosmetic and never change a rule:
  - Merge burst: a merge sends a 4D shell out from the merge point, from the new fruit's radius to 2.6 times it over 1 s. The slice shows its cross-section: a ring that grows, thins and fades with sparks along it and a flash at the centre while young (`drawBursts`). A shell the slice misses is not seen, so turning towards a merge finds its ring. The solver records each merge's position and new fruit (`takeMerges`).
  - Combo: merges within 0.7 s of the last count as a chain; from the second the game shows "COMBO xN" (popping on each merge) and the burst grows to 2.6 times as bright. Scoring is unchanged.
  - Danger: a settled fruit (speed under 1.2 units/s, not just merged) whose centre is within 1.3 of the bowl's rim raises a danger level from 0 (1.3 below) to 1 (0.4 below); a fresh drop above the rim does not count, and the centre is used because a big fruit's top is near the rim anyway. The bowl's lip turns red and pulses in the scene, the screen edge pulses red, and "FULL BOWL" shows from 0.5. The lose rule (a fruit below y = -3) is unchanged.
- Onboarding (first Play, or Tutorial on the title menu; `--tutorial N` starts at step N; F1 skips) teaches the game in 3D first and then reveals the fourth dimension. Steps: 1 aim and drop, 2 merge, 3 turn the slice (A or D; done at 20 degrees), 4 come back (H or Space). While steps 1 and 2 run, the game is plain 3D: the w strip, the slice indicator and tilt label, depth rings, hint rings and merge glow are hidden (`renderer.cues4D`), the 4D keys (turns, W/S slice offset, lane snap) do nothing, and a coach panel at the bottom says what to do. Step 3 switches the 4D cues on, so the reveal is the moment the player turns the slice. If a player never merges, step 2 ends after six drops. Finishing or skipping writes `~/.4dsuika-tutorial-done` (native builds; the web build shows it once per page load).
- The next-drop panel shows the fruit at its true size against the whole merge ladder (cherry to watermelon) with the next fruit marked.
- Merge, score and lose rules stay as they are unless the PI changes them.

## Photo and replay mode
- **P** freezes the game and shows the last 10 s of play (600 snapshots of the fruit and merge bursts, one per frame) as a replay: the slice view on the left and the 4D eye on the right (same camera, same moment), each with its own slice indicator. `Game::RenderPhoto` draws the renderer twice into the two halves of the frame (`renderer.viewportX`), the left in slice view, the right in 4D-eye view, whatever the View setting is.
- Physics is frozen and drops are off, but the camera is live: the usual keys turn the slice in 4D and both halves follow, so a merge can be looked at from other w. `,` and `.` scrub (a tap steps one frame, holding speeds up), Home and End jump, **Enter** plays or pauses the replay (it loops), **F12** saves the current view as a PNG without panels (to `~/Pictures` if it exists, else the home folder; the name carries the date), and **P** or Esc goes back to the game exactly where it was.
- `4d_game --photo N` enters photo mode once N frames are recorded, replaying from the start, for stills (`--screenshot`) and clips (`--record`).

## Proposed next steps (render)
- Turn animation cue: while a 4D turn is in progress, briefly draw the slice's intersection edges (where surfaces cross the old slice) as a fading gold outline, so the player sees what moved in and out.
- Sky disc in the slice: keep the sun's 4D direction fixed in the world but brighten the sky disc when the slice contains it, so turning into w visibly sweeps the sunset.
- Cached sky: bake `skyColor` for the slice into a cubemap with the cache (five 4D value-noise lookups per sky pixel today).
- Temporal accumulation: jitter rays sub-pixel and blend with the previous frame when the camera is still, for clean facet edges at lower render scales.
- Volumetric 4D clouds: cheap noise layers in the sky function marched a few steps in 4D, so clouds reshape when the slice turns.
