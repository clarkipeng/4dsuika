# 4D design contract

## World
- World space is R^4 with coordinates (x, y, z, w); y is up and gravity is -y. Physics (`physics_solver.hpp`) already runs on `glm::vec4` and stays in world space: rotating the view never changes physics.
- Every visible thing is a 4D shape with a signed distance function (SDF) in R^4: fruit are hyperspheres (glomes), the bowl is a 4D hemispherical shell, the island is a 4D terrain plus 4D rocks, trees (spherinder trunks, glome canopies), fences, pond, and clouds. The sky is a function of 4D direction.

## Camera and rotation
- The camera has a position p in R^4 and an orientation R in SO(4) (an orthonormal 4x4 matrix, re-orthonormalized after updates). Its first three columns span the 3D slice the player sees; the fourth is the slice normal.
- What the player sees is the 3D cross-section of the world by the hyperplane through p spanned by R's first three columns. Rotating in the xw, yw and zw planes turns that hyperplane, so the whole scene changes shape; rotating in xy, xz and yz is ordinary 3D turning.
- Controls must make 4D rotation reachable and reversible: a clear "back to the default slice" action, and a small on-screen indicator of the slice's orientation and offset along its normal.

## Rendering
- One fullscreen fragment shader raymarches the 4D SDF scene. Each pixel's ray starts at p and runs along R * (ray direction in slice coordinates, 0), so rays stay inside the slice.
- Shading uses the 4D SDF gradient (normals), a 4D sun direction, soft shadows and ambient occlusion marched in 4D, and fog. Faceted low-poly shading (quantized normals) gives the cover art's look.
- Depth hints for things just off the slice (a faint ghost of nearby fruit, tinted by their offset along the slice normal) are allowed; they must read as hints, not solid objects.
- The same GLSL serves the desktop build and the web build (WebGL2 through emscripten). Keep shaders within GLSL ES 3.00 features.

## Game
- Fruit drop on the bowl from a cursor in the 3D slice; the drop point is mapped to world space through R and p, so the player can aim anywhere in 4D by rotating.
- Merge, score and lose rules stay as they are unless the PI changes them.
