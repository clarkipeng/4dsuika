# 4D design contract

## World
- World space is R^4 with coordinates (x, y, z, w); y is up and gravity is -y. Physics (`physics_solver.hpp`) already runs on `glm::vec4` and stays in world space: rotating the view never changes physics.
- Every visible thing is a 4D shape with a signed distance function (SDF) in R^4: fruit are hyperspheres (glomes), the bowl is a 4D hemispherical shell, the island is a 4D terrain plus 4D rocks, trees (spherinder trunks, glome canopies), fences, pond, and clouds. The sky is a function of 4D direction.

## Camera and rotation
- The camera has a position p in R^4 and an orientation R in SO(4) (an orthonormal 4x4 matrix, re-orthonormalized after updates). Its first three columns span the 3D slice the player sees; the fourth is the slice normal.
- What the player sees is the 3D cross-section of the world by the hyperplane through p spanned by R's first three columns. Rotating in the xw, yw and zw planes turns that hyperplane, so the whole scene changes shape; rotating in xy, xz and yz is ordinary 3D turning.
- Controls must make 4D rotation reachable and reversible: a clear "back to the default slice" action, and a small on-screen indicator of the slice's orientation and offset along its normal.
- Implementation (`src/4d_game/camera4d.hpp`): R = slice * orbit. `orbit` is ordinary yaw and pitch inside the slice (right-drag or arrow keys); `slice` holds every 4D turn and the roll, stored as a pair of unit quaternions so composition, re-orthonormalization and slerp are exact. The camera orbits the bowl: p = target + offset * R[3] + distance * R[2].
- Keys turn in the camera's own planes, so they act on what the player sees: A/D xw, R/F yw, Q/E zw, Z/C roll (xy), W/S move the slice along its normal, H or Space eases back to the default slice. Turns ease in and out, and opposite keys undo each other exactly.
- The indicator (bottom-left) draws each world axis as it appears in the slice: x red, y green, z blue, w gold. An axis tilted out of the slice shrinks and its tip grows a ring, so in the default slice w is a ring at the centre. A gold marker on the bar beside it shows the offset; the text gives the tilt from the default slice in degrees.

## Rendering
- One fullscreen fragment shader raymarches the 4D SDF scene. Each pixel's ray starts at p and runs along R * (ray direction in slice coordinates, 0), so rays stay inside the slice.
- Shading uses the 4D SDF gradient (normals), a 4D sun direction, soft shadows and ambient occlusion marched in 4D, and fog. Faceted low-poly shading (quantized normals) gives the cover art's look.
- A fruit whose cross-section normals lean along the slice normal (it is drifting out of the slice) gains a warm gold rim, the indicator's w colour, without changing its hue.
- Depth hints for things just off the slice (a faint ghost of nearby fruit, tinted by their offset along the slice normal) are allowed; they must read as hints, not solid objects.
- Implemented as a thin ring plus faint fill where the fruit's centre projects into the slice, in the fruit's colour, tinted cool on the +normal side and warm on the − side. Strength fades to zero 1.5 units beyond the slice (16 nearest), and hints behind scene geometry are dimmer.
- The same GLSL serves the desktop build and the web build (WebGL2 through emscripten). Keep shaders within GLSL ES 3.00 features.
- Shader files carry no `#version`; the host prepends it (330 core or 300 es), defines `MAX_FRUIT`, and pastes `#include "world4d.glsl"`.
- `resources/shaders/world4d.glsl` (world) provides `vec2 worldSDF(vec4 p)` (distance, material id), `vec3 skyColor(vec4 dir)` and `vec3 materialColor(float id, vec4 p, vec4 n)`; it may read `uTime` and `uSunDir`. Ids: 1 ground, 2 bowl, 10+ world props; fruit are drawn by the renderer.
- Fruit reach the shader as `uFruitCount`, `uFruitCenters[MAX_FRUIT]` (world vec4) and `uFruitData[MAX_FRUIT]` (radius, type, flash, unused). Game passes all fruit to `RaymarchRenderer::setFruit`; the renderer keeps those whose glome crosses the slice, nearest first, up to 64 (WebGL2's uniform budget).
- Facets quantize the 4D normal to the D4 lattice (24-cell Voronoi cells), so they are fixed to world orientation, cost no extra SDF work, and change as the slice turns. `uFacet` blends smooth to faceted.
- Fruit are hit analytically (ray against glome); only the world is sphere-traced. The scene is marched into an offscreen target at a render scale, then a present pass upscales it and draws the indicator at full resolution.
- Performance: the scene pass is timed with GPU timer queries, and resolution adapts towards an 11 ms scene budget, between half and all of the quality preset's cap. Presets cap the marched pixel count (Low 960×540, Medium 1280×720, High 1920×1080, Ultra native) and set march, shadow and AO budgets; G cycles them, F3 shows the timing. The web build has no timer queries and adapts from frame time.
- `4d_game --screenshot out.png` and `--bench N` run headless (hidden window, no focus, no Dock icon) for PR screenshots and timing; see `main.cpp` for camera and demo flags.

## Game
- Fruit drop on the bowl from a cursor in the 3D slice; the drop point is mapped to world space through R and p, so the player can aim anywhere in 4D by rotating.
- Merge, score and lose rules stay as they are unless the PI changes them.

## Proposed next steps (render)
- Turn animation cue: while a 4D turn is in progress, briefly draw the slice's intersection edges (where surfaces cross the old slice) as a fading gold outline, so the player sees what moved in and out.
- Slice-relative sun: keep the sun's 4D direction fixed in the world but brighten the sky disc when the slice contains it, so turning into w visibly sweeps the sunset.
- Half-resolution lighting: march at full scale, compute shadows and AO at half resolution with a depth-aware upsample; the world SDF is the main cost once the island lands.
- Temporal accumulation: jitter rays sub-pixel and blend with the previous frame when the camera is still, for clean facet edges at lower render scales.
- Drop guide: a thin vertical line from the preview ghost down to its landing point inside the slice.
- Volumetric 4D clouds: cheap noise layers in the sky function marched a few steps in 4D, so clouds reshape when the slice turns.
